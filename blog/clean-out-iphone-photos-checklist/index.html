How to Clean Out iPhone Photos: A 20-Minute Checklist

A practical 20-minute plan for deciding what to keep, what to review, and what can go.

By Emrecan ErOctober 17, 20265 min read

Before you start: protect the photos that matter

A fast cleanup is useful only if you still trust your library afterward. Before deleting, check whether iCloud Photos is on and whether you have a separate, verified copy of irreplaceable photos. With iCloud Photos, deleting in Photos also removes the item from your other devices using that library. Apple's delete and recover guide explains the behavior.

Pick one month, trip, or event. A small boundary keeps this exercise from becoming a risky full-library sweep. If a photo makes you hesitate, leave it for another day.

Minutes 0–5: clear disposable screenshots

In Photos, open Collections and find Screenshots under Media Types. Look for one-time codes, delivery updates, accidental captures, and information you have saved elsewhere. Keep receipts, travel details, and anything you may need to reference later. The bulk screenshot guide explains how to select a group without rushing the final delete.

Do not chase a perfectly empty Screenshots collection. The goal is to remove easy clutter while preserving useful records.

Minutes 5–10: inspect videos and screen recordings

Open Videos and Screen Recordings in Media Types. A few long clips may account for more storage than hundreds of photos. Open a likely large clip and tap Info or swipe up to check its file size. Apple describes this information in its photo and video details guide.

Delete accidental recordings first. For a meaningful video, verify that another copy plays properly before removing it from your phone. Our video storage guide gives you a more careful review method.

Minutes 10–15: review duplicates and near-duplicates

If Photos has identified matches, open Duplicates under Utilities and review each group before merging. Similar shots are a separate decision: a burst may contain different expressions or focus, so choose the version you actually like. See the duplicate photo guide for the difference.

Do not delete every alternate shot just because it looks similar in a thumbnail. Open the finalists full size, then keep the one that captures the moment best.

Minutes 15–20: make the final pass

Review the items selected for deletion. Check faces, documents, and clips that would be difficult to recreate. Confirm only the obvious rejects. Deleted photos and videos normally stay in Recently Deleted for 30 days, but that is a recovery window, not a backup strategy; Apple documents the time limit in its Photos guide.

Stop after 20 minutes, even if the whole library is not finished. Repeat with another month next week. For a more general routine, read how to clean up iPhone photos without losing your favorites.
