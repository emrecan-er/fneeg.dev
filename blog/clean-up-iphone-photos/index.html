Photo cleanup

How to clean up your iPhone photos without losing the keepers.

The goal is a camera roll you can enjoy opening again. That takes a simple decision for each photo and one careful review before you delete.

By Emrecan ErOctober 11, 20263 min read

Most camera rolls do not get messy in one day. They grow a few nearly identical shots at a time: a receipt you no longer need, five photos of the same lunch, a blurry picture between two good ones. Trying to solve it all in one sitting can make every deletion feel risky.

A better routine is short, repeatable, and reversible until the final step. You can use the Photos app, or a focused tool such as Swyper, which presents your library one item at a time and lets you review a cleanup bin before deleting.

Start with one small slice

Pick a recent trip, a month, or a single category. Give yourself ten minutes. Beginning with a limited set makes the choices easier and gives you a useful result even if you stop there.

As you move through it, ask one question: Would I look for this photo again? Keep the best version of a moment. The blurry test shot and the almost identical burst can go. If you are uncertain, leave it for the next pass.

Queue first, delete second

Separating selection from deletion is the most important part of the process. Mark the obvious rejects as you go, then inspect the complete set before confirming. A photo often feels disposable in isolation but meaningful beside the rest of a day.

Swyper follows that pattern: scroll photos full screen, double tap the ones you do not need, and review the cleanup bin. Tapping an item in the bin keeps it. Nothing is deleted when you first mark it.

A useful rule: if you pause for more than a second over a photo, keep it for now. Speed matters less than trusting the result.

Handle duplicates separately

Duplicate files are a different problem from similar photos. Exact copies may be easy to spot, while two shots of the same scene can differ in focus, expression, or framing. Compare them side by side and keep the one you actually prefer. Swyper includes a duplicate finder in Premium, but the judgment about which copy matters is still yours.

Make the routine stick

  1. After an event: keep your favorite shots while the memory is fresh.
  2. Once a week: spend a few minutes on screenshots, accidental captures, and near duplicates.
  3. Before a big delete: review the queue and make sure anything important is backed up in the way you prefer.
